# Strike points — what they are, and the two features waiting on them

A hitbox says what a swing *threatens*. It has never said where the swing
**is**. The box is deliberately generous — a jab's runs from chest to floor so
it catches a crouching opponent (`src/moves.js`) — so its centre is nobody's
fist, and everything that wanted the real point of contact has been guessing:
impact sparks at a fixed offset from the victim's centre, a tipper measured as
a horizontal distance between two centre lines.

A **strike point** is that missing fact: for one fighter, for one move, where
the blow actually lands — the fist, the foot, or the blade. It is baked from
the rigs, checked by a person against the drawing, and stored per character
per state.

## What exists today

**Baked** (`src/config_model_reach.js`, generated). `loader.measureAttackReach`
poses each attack clip at its contact beat *with the aim and reach layers an
unaimed strike carries in game*, then reports the business end of the striking
limb — `ik.js`'s `REACH` table names it per state, and it is a **foot** for the
aerial — or the weapon's far end when a weapon leads the hand. Regenerated by
`tools/derive_attack_envelopes.mjs`; `tools/audit_hitboxes.mjs` fails while the
config is stale, so it cannot quietly describe bodies that no longer exist.

**Overridable** (`src/config_strike_points.js`, hand-editable). A human
decision beats a measurement and survives a model rebuild.

**Resolved** (`src/strike_points.js`). `strikePoint(charKey, state)` returns
`{x, y, source}` in the sim's own frame — `x` forward from the centre line,
`y` in canvas convention so up is negative, the same frame `moves.js` writes
`oy: -92` in. `source` is `"human"`, `"model"` or `"derived"`, so a consumer
that wants to be conservative can decline to act on a point nobody has looked
at. There is always an answer: absent both, it derives one from the fighter's
own measured body.

**Visible.** The debug overlay (backquote) rings the strike point while a
fighter swings, colour-coded by source: cyan verified, amber measured, faint
derived.

**Reviewable.** `/workbench/?edit=verification`, task set `strike-points`,
walks the roster move by move over the real sprite and exports decisions.

## Why the two features below are not built yet

Both change what the player feels, and both are only as good as the point they
measure from. A radial tipper built on a strike point that sits at a fighter's
elbow would quietly retune every spacing in the game, and it would be very
hard to tell from the inside whether the *rule* was wrong or the *point* was.
So: verify first, then build. The gate is two passes through the verification
bench (`/workbench/?edit=verification`):

1. **strike points** — every fighter's `light` and `sideHeavy` at minimum;
2. **centre of mass** — its own task set now, because `COM_BODY_FRAC` (0.55)
   drives tumble rotation, the in-scene 3D pivot, the aim chest line and the
   airborne prone box, and it was chosen rather than measured. `comFrac()`
   in `src/body_points.js` already reads a per-fighter answer and falls back
   to 0.55, so that pass is applied by pasting one block.

Both sets export into the same download, so the gate is one sitting and one
file.

## Follow-up A — the FX tier (contact quality as presentation)

**What.** How cleanly a blow connects drives how it *reads*: a centre-mass
connection gets the heavy thud and the big spark, a graze gets a lighter
scrape, its own sound, and less shake.

**Why this one first.** It changes nothing about who wins. It is the cheapest
way to find out whether the strike points are trustworthy, because a wrong
point shows up immediately as an effect in the wrong place — and if the whole
idea reads badly, reverting is one commit with no balance consequences.

**Where.** `applyHit` in `src/combat.js` already computes a contact point from
the overlap of the swing rect and the victim's hurtbox. Replace that estimate
with the real thing: `strikePoint(owner.charKey, owner.animKey)`, mirrored by
`hb.facing` and offset from the attacker's position, clamped into the victim's
hurtbox. Then derive a `quality` in 0..1 from how far that point sits from the
victim's centre of mass, normalised by their own half-width and half-height so
a broad fighter is not permanently "clean" and a slight one permanently
grazed.

**Then feed presentation only:**

- `hitFx(...)` particle counts and `power` scale with quality; the recipes in
  `src/config_fx.js` already take a power multiplier.
- A graze picks a lighter variant of the element's hit sound
  (`ELEMENT_HIT_SFX`), or the same sound quieter and pitched up.
- `state.camera.shake` and `rumbleFighter` scale a little with it.
- A clean hit on a heavy attack may keep the existing black-flash roll; a
  graze should not roll for it at all.

**Guardrails.** Quality must not touch `dmg`, `baseKb`, `growth`, hitstun or
shield damage in this feature — that is Follow-up B's decision to make
deliberately, not this one's to leak. Skip the whole path when
`strikePoint().source === "derived"`, so a fighter nobody has verified keeps
today's presentation exactly.

**Test.** `tools/smoke_combat.mjs` asserts damage totals are unchanged for a
scripted fight with the tier on and off.

## Follow-up B — the radial tipper

**What.** Replace the sweetspot's one-dimensional horizontal band with the
real distance from the strike point to the victim's body.

**The problem it fixes.** `tipBand` (`src/moves.js`) builds a band at
`tip + inset` and `applyHit` compares it against `Math.abs(target.x - owner.x)`
— a centre-to-centre horizontal distance. That is blind to height entirely: a
naginata's tip band is the same whether Maki's blade is at her opponent's head
or sweeping their shins, and it is measured from a reach number rather than
from the blade. The alignment pass improved the inset (it now uses the
victim's real half-width instead of a flat 30 px) but could not fix the shape.

**The change.** With a verified strike point, the tipper becomes what it
always meant: the distance from where the blade actually is to the nearest
point on the victim's hurtbox. Inside `tolerance` of the authored ring is
sweet; well inside it is sour where the move says so. Weapon fighters' tippers
become geometrically true, including height, and a tip that whiffs over a
crouching opponent stops paying tipper damage.

**Preserve these deliberately.** An authored band (Nanami's 7:3) is a
character's own decision and keeps its own numbers — only *derived* bands
become radial. And the reward must stay a **spacing** mechanic: the distance
that decides sweet-versus-sour should be measured along the swing, not
vertically, or a fighter's height becomes a stealth balance axis nobody chose
and short characters get grazed for existing.

**Where.** `moves.js tipBand` gains a radius rather than a centre-line band;
`combat.js` does the distance test against `hurtbox(target)`; `render.js`
`drawStrikeArcs` and the debug overlay draw the ring around the strike point
instead of two vertical lines.

**Test.** Extend `tools/audit_hitboxes.mjs`: for every fighter with a derived
band, assert the sweet ring is reachable — that the strike point at the
contact beat plus `tolerance` actually intersects a standing hurtbox at
typical spacing. That is the invariant the flat 30 px inset used to violate
(Maki's band once sat 15 px past anything she could connect with).

## Follow-up C — the one that is not scheduled

Bone-capsule collision in `?render=3d` (option B from the original
discussion) — model-shaped hurt volumes instead of rectangles. Still the
"real" answer to visual/gameplay alignment, still a project rather than a
patch, and now cheaper than it was: `measureAttackReach` already proves the
pose-and-measure loop, and the verification bench already provides the
human-in-the-loop surface a capsule fit would need. If it ever starts, start
with **hurt** capsules only — model-shaped targets, box-shaped attacks — which
is the half with the alignment payoff and none of the "my punch whiffed by a
centimetre" frustration.
